              How the numbers are worked out
              Total return
              Price change plus dividends or coupons. Indian indices are the official total-return series from NSE Indices; US and global assets use dividend-adjusted ETF prices.
              Rupee return
              (1 + local return) × (1 + change in USD/INR) − 1. When the rupee weakens, dollar assets earn more in rupees.
              3M return and rank
              Return compounded over the last three months, ranked the same way. An asset near the top on both 1M and 3M is a persistent leader; near the bottom on both, a persistent laggard.
              Rank change
              Last month's rank minus this month's. Positive means the asset moved up.
              Volatility
              Annualised standard deviation of daily returns over the past year.
              Trend stretch
              How far price sits from its 200-day average, measured in units of its own volatility. Beyond ±2 is flagged as stretched. Not shown for cash, which rises every day.
              Gold
              International gold converted to rupees, so it ranks on the same basis as other global assets. Indian gold can differ by several points in a month because of import duty and the local premium.
              World markets
              Each market is a US-listed country fund (for example EWJ for Japan, CQQQ for China tech) with dividends included, converted to rupees. India is the Nifty 50 total-return index. CSI 300 comes from Sina because Yahoo's copy was unreliable.
              Currencies against the rupee
              Rupees per unit of each currency, built on the same USD/INR series used everywhere else. Up means that currency strengthened against the rupee, which helps a rupee investor holding it. The dirham is pegged to the dollar at 3.6725.
              Global yields
              Daily 10-year yields from each official publisher: the Fed (FRED), Bundesbank, Japan's Ministry of Finance, Bank of England and ChinaBond. India's is a monthly average because there is no free daily feed. The data checks compare each daily feed with the OECD's monthly figure.
